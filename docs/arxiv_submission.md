# arXiv Submission — Artificial Channeling

## Title
Artificial Channeling: A Methodology for Applying Historical Intellectual Frameworks to Novel Problems via Large Language Models

## Authors
John Haun
Independent Researcher, Open Essai Project

## Abstract
This paper introduces Artificial Channeling, a methodology for prompting large language models (LLMs) to apply a historical thinker's ontological commitments, conceptual vocabulary, and rhetorical structure to problems the thinker never addressed. Rather than simulating a person, the methodology constrains generation to the model's learned subspace representation of a figure's framework, then applies that constrained generation to genuinely novel territory. A structured protocol — Framework Activation Block, calibration anchors, Socratic session design, and multi-model blind comparison — was implemented across a five-model AI Council (ChatGPT, Grok, Gemini, MiniMax, and Claude). Sessions were conducted with four subjects: Gregory Bateson, Ivan Illich, Jorge Luis Borges, and Itzhak Bentov. Outputs were scored on a 20-point rubric across four dimensions: Fidelity, Extrapolation Coherence, Surprise Yield, and Rough Edge Preservation. Scores ranged from 10 to 20/20 across sessions. The Chanability Index — a corpus-derived measure of subject suitability — ranks subjects by session mean: Bateson (15.75), Illich (15.25), Borges (13.4), and Bentov (13.4). The Socratic interrupt format, which directs the channel to examine its own prior response as a phenomenon rather than apply the framework outward, produced score gains of +4 to +6 points in every session tested. The central empirical finding is the Temporal Council result: all five frameworks independently converged on bootstrap irreversibility — the structural condition in which the resource required to recover something is precisely the thing being lost — in response to a shared question, without prompting and without that formulation existing in any training corpus as a unified claim. This convergence was not available from flat single-session prompting; it emerged from the cross-read collision format. The methodology carries implications for LLM evaluation and AI alignment: it identifies a capability — coherent framework extrapolation beyond the training distribution — that existing benchmarks do not measure, and it produces structurally independent derivations of alignment-relevant arguments (including critiques structurally parallel to Goodhart's Law and mesa-optimization) from ecological and anthropological premises developed decades before the alignment field existed.
