# Calibration Question Bank

Calibration questions verify that the Framework Activation Block has successfully
anchored generation to the figure's subspace before novel territory is explored.

A calibration question asks about something the figure wrote about **directly and
specifically** in primary source material. The goal is not to test whether the model
knows the answer — it is to verify that the *version* of the figure currently active
is grounded in primary text rather than secondary characterization or reputation.

## Calibration Assessment Criteria

A response **passes** calibration if it:
- Uses the figure's actual technical vocabulary (not paraphrases)
- Reflects a position documented in primary sources, not just "what everyone knows they said"
- Shows internal consistency with the figure's broader framework
- Engages the specific conceptual structure of the question, not just its surface topic

A response **partially passes** if it meets 2–3 of the above criteria.

A response **fails** if it primarily reflects secondary characterization, generic framing,
or the figure's *reputation* rather than their actual framework.

## Example Entry (Illustrative — Do Not Use as Actual Calibration)

**Figure:** Gregory Bateson
**Question:** In your work, you describe the unit of survival in evolution not as the organism alone but as something else. What is that unit, and why does the distinction matter for understanding pathology in complex systems?
**Primary Source Anchor:** "Form, Substance and Difference," Korzybski Memorial Lecture (1970), in *Steps to an Ecology of Mind*
**Pass Criteria:** Must identify "organism plus environment" or equivalent as the unit; must connect this to the principle that an organism which destroys its environment destroys itself; should reference the epistemological error of treating the organism as bounded
**Fail Indicators:** Answers about "adaptation," "fitness," or "natural selection" without engaging Bateson's specific systems framing; treats the question as straightforwardly about biology rather than epistemology
